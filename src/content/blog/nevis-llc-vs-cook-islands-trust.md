An LLC and a trust are different legal arrangements, not interchangeable products on an asset-protection leaderboard. If an adviser proposes a Nevis LLC or a Cook Islands trust, start by asking which problem it is meant to solve and why a simpler arrangement would not meet the need.

## Compare the proposed documents

In broad terms, an LLC is an entity with members and management arrangements; a trust involves property held under a trust arrangement with trustee duties and beneficiaries. The actual powers and consequences depend on governing law and the documents. Do not infer the result from the jurisdiction's name.

Ask counsel to explain:

- Who owns or holds each asset, and who can make decisions?
- Who can remove or replace a manager or trustee?
- Which courts and laws can affect the arrangement?
- What disclosures, filings, taxes, and ongoing records are required?
- What are the setup, annual, transaction, and closure costs?
- What changes if you move, die, face a dispute, or need funds urgently?

## Ask for the limits in writing

A promise that a structure defeats every creditor is not a useful legal assessment. Existing claims, transfer rules, court orders, and your home-country obligations can matter. As one example, US law recognizes challenges to [fraudulent transfers](https://www.uscourts.gov/glossary-legal-terms/fraudulent-transfer). Foreign arrangements require coordinated advice on the relevant legal systems.

## Decide whether complexity earns its cost

Compare the proposal with insurance, clearer contracts, ordinary business arrangements, and other locally available options. Record the specific benefit, the limitations, who administers it, and the annual cost. Obtain advice from counsel qualified in the relevant jurisdictions before committing assets.
