Remote work can connect you to several countries at once. A useful plan makes the relevant facts visible before deadlines arrive. Here are seven checks to work through with the appropriate adviser.

## 1. Assess residence instead of assuming it

Traveling frequently does not establish that no country can tax you. Apply the relevant rules to your homes, work, family ties, and days. Your tax position follows those rules rather than a status you simply choose.

## 2. Review the departure year

Ask what changes when you leave, what continues, and which returns or notifications apply. Do not close accounts or dispose of property because of a generic checklist. See the [tax residence review worksheet](/blog/how-to-become-tax-non-resident/).

## 3. Check permission for your activities

Confirm whether your immigration status permits the work you intend to do. Visitor and remote-work rules differ. Use the destination's official requirements rather than assuming that a tourist entry either permits or prohibits every kind of remote activity.

## 4. Keep a travel record

Record entries, exits, accommodation, and work locations. Keep supporting documents and ask how the relevant system counts days. A single 183-day shortcut is not a complete residence assessment.

## 5. Keep business records usable

Separate and classify transactions, retain invoices, and reconcile accounts. Clear records help whoever prepares your returns understand what happened without reconstructing the year from memory.

## 6. Include obligations beyond income tax

Ask about social contributions, payroll, business registration, and indirect taxes where relevant. A treaty or relief may have conditions and a process; do not assume it applies automatically.

## 7. Check reporting separately from tax owed

US FBAR reporting can apply when aggregate reportable foreign accounts exceed $10,000 at any time in a calendar year, subject to applicable rules and exceptions. A small tax bill does not decide that filing question. [IRS FBAR guidance](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/report-of-foreign-bank-and-financial-accounts-fbar).

Put the answers into a calendar with an owner, due date, and required records for each task. Explore [international banking basics](/blog/offshore-banking-for-beginners/) and the [Freedom Library](/reading-guide/) for related background reading.
