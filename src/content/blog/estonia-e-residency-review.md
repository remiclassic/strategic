Estonia's e-Residency program offers a digital identity that can support remote business administration. Its value depends on whether that model fits your company and whether you can meet the resulting obligations.

## Know what the status provides

It is not citizenship, a travel document, or permission to live in Estonia. It also does not automatically establish your personal tax residence. Start with the [official explanation of e-Residency](https://learn.e-resident.gov.ee/hc/en-gb/articles/360000711978-What-is-e-Residency).

## Model payments as well as retained profit

Estonian distributed-profit corporate tax uses 22/78 of the net distribution from 2025. Salary, benefits, and foreign obligations need separate assessment. A statement about retained profit does not describe every transaction your company might make. [Estonian Tax and Customs Board](https://www.emta.ee/en/business-client/taxes-and-payment/income-and-social-taxes/income-tax-and-basic-exemption).

Discuss where you work and manage the company, how you pay yourself, and whether other countries impose duties. The program's own guidance explains that [tax obligations can arise abroad](https://www.e-resident.gov.ee/es/blog/posts/how-do-e-residents-pay-taxes/).

## Verify the practical setup

Request current quotes for application, company registration, address or contact services where required, accounting, and annual filings. Confirm bank or payment-provider eligibility directly. A digital identity and a company registration do not guarantee an account.

Compare the cost and workflow with a local arrangement using the same transaction volumes and owner-payment needs. Ask who handles each filing and how you would close the company if the model stopped fitting.

## Decide from the operating plan

The useful question is whether remote administration solves a real business need after all costs and responsibilities. Document that benefit before applying.
