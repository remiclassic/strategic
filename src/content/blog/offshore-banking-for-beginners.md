An account abroad can help with a concrete task: receiving a customer's currency, paying local expenses, or operating where your business has a presence. Start with that task. Opening an account is not, on its own, a tax strategy or a guarantee that money is protected.

## Write an account brief

Give a potential provider a clear description of your business, owners, residence, expected payment countries, currencies, and monthly volumes. Ask which documents are needed and whether your activities are supported before committing to a setup service.

Compare the total annual cost: opening and maintenance fees, minimum-balance conditions, transfers, currency conversion, cards, and any intermediary charges. Keep a dated fee schedule. A provider that works for one resident or entity may not accept another.

## Understand what you are opening

Check the provider's regulated legal entity and the type of account. An app, payments institution, and bank are not interchangeable. Ask how funds are held, what protection applies, what its limits are, and what happens if either the provider or a banking partner fails.

The FDIC's explanation of [third-party financial apps](https://www.fdic.gov/consumer-resource-center/2024-06/banking-third-party-apps) is a useful US example of why the underlying arrangement matters.

## Include reporting from the start

For US persons, FBAR can apply when the aggregate value of reportable foreign accounts exceeds $10,000 at any time during the calendar year, subject to applicable rules and exceptions. This is not a separate $10,000 allowance for each account. Tax-return disclosures may also apply. [IRS: FBAR](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/report-of-foreign-bank-and-financial-accounts-fbar).

Other countries have their own reporting and tax obligations. Keep statements, ownership records, and source-of-funds documentation organized.

## Make access resilient

Use strong account security, maintain current contact details, and know your support and complaint routes. Document an alternative way to receive essential customer payments. Review the arrangement as your business changes.
