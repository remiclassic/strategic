Before approaching a Swiss institution, define the service you need: everyday payments, securities custody, or investment management. These can have different eligibility requirements, fee schedules, and protections. A country's reputation cannot replace the terms of a specific account.

## Confirm fit before applying

Ask whether the institution accepts your country of residence, citizenship, entity type, and intended activity. Request a current written quote, including any minimum relationship balance, non-resident surcharge, custody costs, transfer fees, and investment-management charges.

Prepare identity, address, ownership, and source-of-funds records. Do not assume that satisfying an advertised minimum guarantees account approval.

## Identify the protection

Check the institution's authorization and ask which assets are deposits and which are held in custody. FINMA describes enhanced protection for deposits up to CHF 100,000 at authorized banks and securities firms in bankruptcy. The details and type of assets matter; it is not a blanket guarantee for every investment or balance. [FINMA: depositor protection](https://www.finma.ch/en/supervision/banks-and-securities-firms/depositor-protection/).

## Put the account in your wider plan

Ask your adviser which home-country tax and reporting duties apply. Consider currency exposure, access to funds, and the practicality of resolving a problem remotely. Compare the same services with eligible institutions closer to home before deciding that an international account is worth its administration.

Your decision sheet should include the purpose, eligible institution, complete annual cost, protections, reporting owner, and exit process. Leave any unanswered questions open until you have documentary support.
